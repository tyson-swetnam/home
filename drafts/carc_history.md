# A History of CARC and the Galles Building

The University of New Mexico's [Center for Advanced Research Computing](https://carc.unm.edu){target=_blank} (CARC) keeps its supercomputers in an unlikely place: a mid-century car dealership on Central Avenue, Albuquerque's stretch of historic Route 66. This is a short history of the building and the center that grew inside it.

## The Galles Motor Company

The Galles family had been in the transportation business in New Mexico for generations. It began with a wagon-repair shop; in 1908, H.R. Galles brought the first automobile to Albuquerque and went on to sell the city its first eleven cars. In 1933 the company took on the Cadillac, Oldsmobile, and Chevrolet franchises, and under H.L. Galles, Jr. it grew into one of the state's largest employers, with dealerships across New Mexico and beyond.

In the mid-1950s the family built a new showroom for its Cadillac and Oldsmobile lines on Central Avenue, just west of the university. Local architect [William Burk, Jr.](https://albuquerquemodernism.unm.edu/posts/cs10_galles_motor_company.html){target=_blank} designed it in the confident modern idiom of the decade: a sunburst-shaped showroom wrapped in Arizona stone and a glass curtain wall, the Cadillac and Oldsmobile badges cut in low relief into the façade. That showroom was only the bright tip of a much larger building — some seventy-three thousand square feet, most of it given over to the mechanics' bays where a dealership made its steadiest money.

## An unlikely home for a supercomputer

UNM acquired the building and, in 1994, established the Albuquerque Resource Center there — soon renamed the Albuquerque High Performance Computing Center (AHPCC), part of the broader High Performance Computing Education and Research Center. In 2005 a major renovation carved a state-of-the-art machine room out of the old service bays. The center took its present name, the Center for Advanced Research Computing, in 2009.

Remnants of the dealership survive throughout: the badges in the stone, and a hand-painted map of New Mexico that still watches over what was once the showroom floor.

## Art among the machines

That same 2005 renovation made room for more than computers. It created studio space for the ARTS Lab — Art, Research, Technology, and Science — and in 2011 the CARC Gallery opened, exhibiting work by students, faculty, and alumni a few steps from the machine room. Computation and culture have shared the building ever since.
